# Limitations and responsible-use boundaries

- MAP covers 15 middle-school math questions, primarily rational numbers and equations/functions; this is not a representative K–12 sample.
- The corpus includes synthetic paraphrases (27% in the benchmark paper), so response-level results may partially reflect paraphrase families rather than independent student reasoning.
- The taxonomy records a single human-assigned code per response. Student reasoning can be ambiguous or contain multiple errors.
- The published paper reports no student demographics, so subgroup fairness cannot be assessed from this corpus.
- Grouped folds test within-benchmark item transfer, not deployment shift across schools, curricula, dialects, or new modalities. Some questions may require visual information not represented in a text-only model.
- Confidence is an empirical score, not a guarantee of correctness. Calibration may fail under new distributions.
- “Human review” results are simulations. They do not measure educator agreement, usability, workload, cost, or learning impact.
- Raw student text and derived per-row outputs remain non-public unless authoritative terms explicitly allow release.
